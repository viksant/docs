# Office Hours

Your team isn't online 24/7, and that's fine. Set your **office hours** and BestChatBot fits human handoff around them. Inside your hours, a visitor connects to an operator live. Outside them, the same request becomes an **async ticket** and the visitor gets an email when your team replies. The bot keeps answering while they wait, so no one stares at a blank screen.

<Note>Setting office hours doesn't depend on your plan. Any member with an **Editor** role or higher can set them. Live handoff itself still needs **operator seats**, which come with your plan. See [Human Handoff](/live-chat/human-handoff).</Note>

## Setting Your Hours

Open **Web Widget → Availability**. By default the schedule is off and operators show as available around the clock. Turn on **Use an availability schedule** to limit live support to set hours.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/office-hours-availability-tab.png" alt="Availability tab with the schedule toggle, workspace timezone, and weekly hours editor" />
</Frame>

Two settings do the work:

- **Workspace timezone** sets the clock your hours run on, not the visitor's and not your browser's. Point it at wherever your team works, like Europe/Madrid.
- **Weekly schedule** takes one or more time ranges per day. A day with no ranges stays **closed**. Ranges use 24-hour time and can't cross midnight.

<Warning>Turn the schedule on but leave every day empty, and operators show as offline all week. Email then answers **every** request. Add at least one range, or turn the schedule off for 24/7 live support.</Warning>

## What Visitors See

The **Talk to a human** button looks the same inside and outside your hours. Only what happens on tap changes. It disappears just once: when you have no operator capacity at all.

- **Inside your hours**, the conversation connects live. The visitor reads *"Connecting you with our team. Someone will be with you shortly."* and your operators get a ping.
- **Outside your hours**, the request opens an async ticket. The visitor reads *"We're outside our support hours right now. We'll email you when we reply."* An anonymous visitor also gets a short form to leave an email, so you can reach them.

## The Waiting Ticket

An after-hours request, or a live request nobody grabs in time, becomes a **waiting** ticket. It's a real, open handoff:

- Any operator can claim and answer it later, from the dashboard, Slack, or Discord.
- The bot keeps answering the visitor while it sits, so they're never stuck.
- It stays open for up to **30 days**. If nobody ever picks it up, it quietly goes back to the bot.

In your dashboard [live inbox](/live-chat/human-handoff), these show a **Waiting** status next to your live conversations.

## Emailing the Visitor

Email is how a visitor avoids sitting and waiting. BestChatBot emails the visitor in two cases. Here's each one:

- **They reach you after hours.** A *"We got your message"* email confirms your team will follow up. BestChatBot sends it only to visitors whose email is **verified** through [signed identity](/integrations/identity/overview), never to an address typed into the chat. We built that guard on purpose, to stop misuse.
- **You reply and they've already left.** If the visitor closed the chat, BestChatBot emails your reply as *"You have a new reply from support"* with a preview. It caps at one email per conversation every 15 minutes.

Every visitor email accepts a reply. The visitor answers the email and their message lands right back in the conversation, so the thread keeps going after they close the widget.

<Note>Visitor emails are best-effort. If delivery fails, the handoff still works, the visitor just misses that one notification. A visitor who never shares an email stays in the widget only.</Note>
